# BACKEND_GAPS

> What the Canadian Elder Life Story handoff asks for that this platform cannot yet
> serve. Kept as the front end is built, not written up afterwards, on the owner's
> instruction: **build the front end first, record the missing back end for later.**
>
> Status started 2026-08-22. A row leaves this file when an endpoint exists **and** a
> screen reads from it — not when either half alone is done.

## How the front end behaves while a row here is open

Every screen built against a gap says so on the screen, in the participant's words, and
offers nothing it cannot honour. That is not politeness: this project has removed a
control that promised what the platform could not deliver more than once (D-2, D-5,
D-21, D-34, D-75), and the consent screen was found offering six withdrawals that were
certain to fail (D-101). A design handed over complete is exactly the circumstance in
which that mistake gets made again, at speed.

## Gaps

| # | What the design needs | What exists | What is missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| B-1 | **Story entries** — list of a participant's own memories with title, excerpt, and provenance meta ("Your words, spoken · 3 min · June 2026" / "Anne's account · accepted May 2026") | M17 Life Story holds items and contributions | A query returning entries with **provenance and duration** in the shape the list needs; nothing records "spoken" against "written", and nothing records an audio length |
| B-2 | **Speech capture** — record audio, show an elapsed clock, then "We will type this out for you" | nothing | Audio upload, storage against a life-story item, and transcription. R2 holds bytes; there is no audio pipeline and no transcription provider (ADR-109 is unchosen) |
| B-3 | **Photographs on a memory** — attach, caption, and "take the photograph out" | R2 object storage is connected and exercised | Image upload from the browser, a per-item photo relation, a caption field, and removal. **Malware scanning is still a simulator** (ADR-126) — nothing may claim a photograph has been checked |
| ~~B-4~~ | ~~The uncaptioned-photo prompt~~ | `caption` / `caption_written_at` on `storage_ops.stored_objects`, `captionObject` behind a new `object.caption` action, and `listUncaptionedPhotographs`; Home card and `caption` screen | **Closed 2026-08-29.** Nullable because null *is* the state the card exists to find; an empty caption clears back to null rather than storing emptiness that would read as answered. | B-11 | **Messages as conversations** — thread rows open to a conversation | M07 delivery and threads exist | The design opens a thread to a toast; a real conversation view needs the existing thread queries wired up |

| B-13 | **Helper mode, decisions deferred** — "those decisions are put aside and shown to you again once helping stops" | `AssistedMode` is read-only assistance (D-15) | Deferring a decision while a helper is present, and re-presenting it afterwards. D-15 ruled read-only assistance deliberately; this is a change to that ruling, not an addition |
| ~~B-14~~ | ~~Recent decisions~~ | `listMyRecentDecisions` (M15) over `governance_audit.audit_events`, served at `GET /v1/participants/:id/decisions` | **Closed 2026-08-29.** The store had sixty-one writers and one reader — the staff view, behind `audit.view`, which no participant holds. This is the second reader and cannot become the first: the actor is the caller and is not a parameter, the actions are an allow-list of decisions rather than everything logged, and `result = 'Succeeded'` keeps refused attempts out. Reading it is deliberately not itself audited, unlike `listAuditEvents` — nobody can look at anybody else through it, and Home loads it every visit. The phrases stop where the record stops: an audit row does not say which way a contribution review went, so nothing claims it did |
| ~~B-15~~ | ~~A brand mark~~ | the owner's `icareu` mark and wordmark, source in `design/brand/`, inlined in `apps/web/src/components/elder/BrandMark.tsx` and shipped as `apps/web/public/favicon.svg` | **Closed 2026-08-29.** It arrived on this project's own icon grid — 24×24 at stroke-width 2, the same as `TabIcon` — and its `#b68235` is exactly `--cl-accent`, so nothing was reinterpreted. Still missing: an `apple-touch-icon`, which wants a square 180×180 raster and neither supplied PNG is square |
| ~~B-16~~ | ~~A name to greet~~ | `getMyProfile` (M02) behind `participant.view-own`, served at `GET /v1/participants/:id/profile` | **Closed 2026-08-29.** The name had been in `participant_profile.participants.display_name` since M02's first migration; what was missing was a way for its owner to read it. Null is still a real answer — a participant with no profile row is a normal synthetic-setup state — and the greeting then stands on its own rather than falling back to the identifier |
| ~~B-17~~ | ~~Who offered this~~ | the contributor's account id from M17, turned into a name by M01's `AccountNameQueryPort` in the controller | **Closed 2026-08-29 by an owner ruling that reverses an earlier one.** The query used to withhold the contributor because naming them in a list "would let anyone enumerate who has been writing about them". Weighed against it: the endpoint is owner-only, so "anyone" was never the caller but whoever else can see the screen; and somebody deciding whether another person's words enter their own life story needs to know who wrote them more than the list needs the discretion. Both sides of the trade are recorded in the query's own comment. An account with no name shows as a stated gap, never as an identifier |
| B-18 | **Helper mode defers decisions** — the design's `helper` screen says "those decisions are put aside and shown to you again once helping stops" | D-15's read-only assistance, and B-13 | Same gap as B-13, seen from the screen that promises it. The sentence is **not printed**: it is the one thing somebody is relying on while another person reads their screen, and nothing in the platform does it. What the screen says instead is what is true — the decisions stay theirs and nothing is held back |
| B-19 | **Showing the photograph on the caption screen** — the design puts the picture above the field | nothing serves a released object's bytes to a client | The screen asks somebody to say who is in a photograph it cannot show them. Quarantined bytes are deliberately never served; what is missing is the released path. Until it exists the screen says the picture cannot be shown rather than leaving a blank space that reads as a failure to load |
| B-20 | **"Or use the code from your letter"** — the design's sign-in offers a field and a **Continue with my code** button | Supporter invitations are keyed on **email address**, not on a code: `identity_org.account_invitations` has `invited_email` and no code column, and `findPendingInvitation` matches `(issuer, email)` | The owner says the letter carries a supporter invitation code (2026-08-30). The built platform has no such code — an invited supporter redeems by pressing **the same Sign in with Google button** and being matched on the address they were invited at. So the field is not drawn: there is nothing to type into it. **The real defect it points at is different and worse**: a supporter who signs in here lands on "this account does not have a participant workspace… use the supporter entrance", which is a dead end with no way to get there on a deployment where the surfaces have separate addresses. Routing an account with no participant record but an approved relationship straight to the supporter workspace is the fix, and it is a change to how sign-in routes rather than a field on a form |
| ~~B-22~~ | ~~A telephone number somebody can actually ring~~ | A message box on the about screen, posting to a relay of the owner's own design | **Closed 2026-08-31** by removing the number rather than finding a real one (owner). The 555-01xx placeholder rang nobody; the box reaches somebody. What was given up is stated on the screen rather than glossed: the card promised a person answering between eight in the morning and eight at night, and nothing answers a written message at a stated time |
| B-23 | **High contrast reaches the participant workspace** — the display-preferences panel offers a high-contrast setting, and `:root[data-contrast='high']` defines a full strengthened ramp for it | The setting is stored, the attribute is set on `:root`, and for the participant workspace **none of it applies** | `[data-workspace='participant']` declares the semantic tokens **on the element itself** — first to pin the platform's light values so the workspace cannot follow a dark device, then to remap a subset to the Classical ramp — and a custom property set on an element always beats the same property inherited from an ancestor. So the root's high-contrast values are shadowed regardless of selector specificity. **Widened on 2026-08-31**: pinning against dark extended this from the remapped subset to every token the dark theme touches, which is the cost of fixing a workspace that went black on a dark phone. The toolbar's own contrast button is narrower still — it sets surface and text on `[data-elder-content]`, the reading region only, so it never reaches the toolbar or the tab bar. **This is a control the platform does not honour** (D-2/D-5/D-21), offered to the audience most likely to need it. The fix is a decision about how the Classical ramp and the contrast ramp compose, which is the owner's to make |
| B-24 | **Somebody reads the messages, and something says when** | A relay that delivers them | Nothing here knows who receives a message, how quickly, or what happens if nobody does. The screen is careful not to promise a time, which is honest and is not the same as an answer. Before participants use this, the study needs a named person, a checked destination, and a rule for the case nobody has thought about yet: a message that says somebody is in trouble, arriving at four in the morning at an inbox nobody is watching |
| B-25 | **Where a participant's words go when they leave the platform** | The API relays them to a third party outside this platform's audit trail, permission engine and data-protection review | Narrower than when first recorded, because the platform now does the sending itself and could log, rate-limit or refuse — but the destination is still outside it. Everything a participant writes elsewhere is governed: who may read it, what is recorded, what a copy request returns. A message typed into this box is none of those, and the person writing it may well include health information, distress or somebody else's name because the screen invited them to write freely. It needs a line in SECURITY_AND_PRIVACY_PLAN and THREAT_MODEL, and it needs to be true of whatever the destination actually is |
| B-26 | **A route for somebody in immediate difficulty** | The safety panel, and a message box that says nothing about urgency | The screen this box sits on is one somebody reaches when they are stuck, and the telephone that used to be here is gone. A written message is the wrong instrument for a crisis and this one does not claim otherwise, but "does not claim" is not "provides". What is missing is the sentence naming a real crisis line, which is a thing to obtain rather than to write |
| ~~B-27~~ | ~~Nothing on this platform could read a stored file back~~ | `readObject` + `GET /v1/objects/:objectId/content`, owner-scoped | **Closed 2026-09-01.** Every other part of the pipeline existed — initiate, complete, scan, release, list, delete — so a participant could add a photograph to a memory and the screen could only ever say "image/jpeg · 412 KB · added Tuesday": a filing card where the picture should be, on the one screen whose whole subject is somebody's own life. The route serves a content type sniffed from the bytes rather than the one declared at upload, so a file that claims to be a photograph and is markup cannot be served as an image on this origin; `nosniff` alongside it, `no-store` because the classification is Sensitive-Personal, and the same `object.view-own` the listing already used, so it adds a way to read a file and no new right to read one |
| B-28 | **The format an iPhone takes photographs in** | JPEG, PNG, MP3, PDF and plain text | A phone set to "High Efficiency" — the default — produces HEIC, which is refused at the gate. The refusal is clean and readable, which is the least this should do, and it still means somebody is told their own photograph is not an acceptable kind of file. Accepting it is not a one-line allowlist change: the read route serves an image only for bytes it recognises, HEIC would need its own signature, and Chrome cannot display HEIC even when Safari can — so a file that uploaded would then be undisplayable for most people, which is worse than being told no. What it needs is a decision: convert in the browser before upload, or say on the screen which formats work and how to change the phone setting |
| B-29 | **Something has to run the scheduled sweeps** — *object scanning and expiry closed; reconciliation still open* | Scanning happens on upload; expiry is worked out when somebody asks | Owner's rulings: no worker (2026-09-02), check a file when it arrives, and judge expiry at read time rather than by a sweep. **Scanning**: `completeUpload` scans in the same act — the object is still written Quarantined and scanned from there, the CHECK constraint still refuses Available without a clean scan, and a failed scan still never marks anything safe; only the timing moved. **Expiry**: it turned out the deciding paths were already read-time — the policy engine compares a relationship's expiry against the clock every time it evaluates, the messaging commands do the same, match candidates and mutual acceptances refuse when past their date and are filtered out of listings. What was NOT read-time was the *description*: the screen answering "who has access to my information" read the stored column and reported a lapsed access as Active. That is now derived (`relationshipStateNow`), on both the participant's list and the supporter's, with a guard that fails if a future reader hands the stored state on unchanged. **What is still open:** reconciliation, which is not expiry and cannot be derived on read — Delivery Unknown is never reconciled (a message whose delivery is unknown stays unknown rather than being moved after a stale period, ADR-124), and stale outbox claims are never returned to Pending, so an outbox row whose publisher crashed mid-flight is never retried (ADR-015 at-least-once). Both need something to run, or a different design |
| B-30 | **Sharing a life story** — *supporters closed 2026-09-02; connections and communities still open* | A supporter reads what was shared with supporters, words and photographs | Was: visibility stored on the item, audited when it changes, and read by nothing — so a participant set a memory to Community, was told their community could see it, and nobody could reach it. Now built for one scope end to end: the `My Supporters` scope, the standing rule (`mayRead`, pure and exhaustive), the standing query (approved and unexpired relationships only, expiry checked at read time because the sweep does not run — B-29), `life-story.view-shared` and `object.view-shared` which grant the attempt and never the content, the shared-story query and route, a photograph that follows the memory it is on through a port so M16 never learns what a life story is, the chooser on the participant's side and the reading screen on the supporter's. `Connections` and `Community` are carried by the community feed built 2026-09-03 — "Other people's stories" — which resolves whose stories a viewer may see as sets rather than one query per participant, includes the reader's own shared pieces marked as theirs, and refuses drafts, withdrawn pieces and anything Private. **What is still open:** nothing of the sharing itself. `Selected People` is B-31. The owner's ruling that a recorded scope takes effect is satisfied by construction: the read path reads the stored visibility, so a memory already marked Community becomes readable the moment a screen for it exists |
| B-31 | **"Selected People" has nobody to select** | A scope a participant can choose, and no table of who was chosen | There is no per-item list of named people anywhere on this platform — `life_story.access_grants` exists and nothing writes it except a test. So choosing "Selected People" shares a memory with nobody, which `standingOf` now says by returning `isSelected: false` permanently: failing closed rather than quietly widening to some other group. Found while building B-30. It needs either a way to name people per memory, or the scope removed from what the screen offers — a control that cannot do the thing it names is the failure this project keeps taking out |
| B-32 | **A message cannot be taken back, archived, or expire** | `messages.lifecycle_state` allows nine values; three are reachable | Only `Draft`, `Queued` and `Sent` are ever written — `Confirmed for Send`, `Sending`, `Withdrawn`, `Cancelled`, `Expired` and `Archived` are in the column's CHECK and no command writes any of them. So a participant who sends something they regret has no way to withdraw it, and nothing ages out of a conversation. Found while building the conversation list: the first version of its preview rule withheld the words of a `Withdrawn` message — a guard written against a state nothing can reach, while the reachable case it got wrong was `Queued`, which is where every real message rests when no delivery provider is configured, so every conversation would have shown a blank preview. The rule is now an allow-list of states in which somebody has said the words, which is safe for whichever of these is built first, but the commands themselves are missing |
| B-33 | **A participant cannot correct the name on their own research record** | `participants.display_name`, written once at registration and never again | For somebody who signed in with Google it is whatever Google had, copied by `selfRegister` with nobody reviewing it; for somebody a coordinator registered it is what the coordinator typed. There is no command to change it — `participant.update-own` exists and writes only accessibility preferences. The first-arrival screen shows it and does not offer to edit it (X-46): rewriting a research record is a change to research data and a first-run screen with no reviewer is the wrong place for it. What is needed is a decision about who may correct that name and on whose authority, and then a command for it |
| ~~B-21~~ | ~~"about" in the footer and "About this project" on Help~~ | `AboutScreen`, reached from the footer on every screen — the sign-in screen included — and from Help | **Closed 2026-08-31**, built from the rendered prototype |
