1Canadian Elder Life Story Project — mobile app

Private by default, one decision at a time.

Built on the Classical system. Two departures I made deliberately, both for legibility at 78: body copy is set flush left rather than justified, and every size is stepped up from the system's interface scale — 18px body, 62px tap targets. Interactive text uses accent-700, not the base gold, which only reaches 3:1 on this ground.

Assumptions from your answers: Margaret, 78, Halifax. Her daughter Anne helps on Margaret's own device, no second login. Nothing enters her story unless she accepts it, and an accepted contribution is always shown as Anne's account. Photographs are grey labelled placeholders — I can't generate images.

1a is live — tap through it. 1b and 1c are the two alternate Home screens.

2Where the training exercises go

Three placements. My recommendation is 2b as the permanent home with 2a's prompt appearing only in the week the study asks for a reading — the exercises belong to the research relationship, not to the life story and not to the community. I have called them exercises rather than games throughout: a Parkinson's tapping measure framed as a game invites a score, and a score invites a person to read a bad day as decline.

About this project

icareu is a place for older people in Canada to write down their own life story, in their own words, and to read the stories of others who choose to share them.

It is run by the Healthy Aging Intelligence Lab. Taking part is voluntary, and stopping does not affect any care or service you receive.

How your story is treated
