## 1. Purpose

This document defines the current research programme for the Healthy Aging Digital Intervention Research Platform.

The programme studies whether the proposed concepts, intervention mechanisms, domain model and architecture form a coherent and useful theoretical framework.

It does not recruit people, deliver an intervention to real participants or collect identifiable human-subject data.

---

## 2. Current Research Status

The programme is active and may proceed immediately.

No research ethics, institutional governance, provider, clinical or production approval is required for the current scope because the work is limited to theory, public or supplied sources, synthetic data, simulations and isolated prototypes.

---

## 3. Boundary with Future Empirical Research

A future human-subject study would be a separate project.

It would require its own question, protocol, sample, consent process, data plan and any processes applicable in its setting.

Nothing in this document authorises or initiates such a study, and its possible future existence does not delay the current conceptual programme.

---

## 4. Research Object

The primary research object is the proposed relationship between:

- Healthy Aging concepts;
- Participant autonomy;
- identity and Life Story;
- meaningful human connection;
- governed Community;
- Open Matching;
- AI-assisted burden reduction;
- research reproducibility;
- and the digital architecture required to preserve these ideas.

---

## 5. Primary Objective

Develop, challenge and refine a coherent conceptual and executable model for Participant-controlled Healthy Aging digital interventions.

---

## 6. Secondary Objectives

1. Clarify constructs and boundaries.
2. Make causal and mechanism assumptions explicit.
3. Formalise domain invariants and state transitions.
4. Identify contradictions and missing concepts.
5. Explore architecture consequences.
6. Create reproducible synthetic scenarios.
7. Generate future empirical propositions without presenting them as established effects.

---

## 7. Primary Research Questions

1. Is the framework internally coherent?
2. Are Healthy Aging outcomes separated from platform activity?
3. Do the proposed intervention mechanisms logically connect activities to future outcomes?
4. Can Participant control be represented consistently across Consent, Visibility, Life Story, matching, messaging and AI?
5. Are the M01–M18 boundaries sufficient to preserve authority and research lineage?

---

## 8. Secondary Research Questions

1. Which concepts remain ambiguous or overlapping?
2. Which mechanisms depend on untested empirical assumptions?
3. Which architecture decisions follow from the theory and which are optional implementation choices?
4. Which synthetic failure scenarios reveal contradictions?
5. Which future observations would most reduce uncertainty?

---

## 9. Theoretical Propositions

The programme may examine propositions such as:

- Participant control is a mechanism, not merely a compliance property.
- Life Story can support identity continuity only when authorship and audience remain controllable.
- digital connection has value only when it supports meaningful human interaction rather than engagement volume;
- matching requires independent choice and cannot be inferred from system activity;
- AI can reduce interaction burden while preserving human authority if its actions remain transparent and bounded;
- research reproducibility requires separate records for data, analysis, interpretation and findings.

---

## 10. Epistemic Classification

Every statement or result is labelled as one of:

- definition;
- source-derived statement;
- deductive consequence;
- design assumption;
- simulated observation;
- prototype observation;
- inference;
- speculative proposition;
- contradiction;
- or future empirical question.

---

## 11. Source Basis

The programme uses:

- Documents 0–20 and Appendices A–F;
- sources explicitly supplied with the project;
- public literature only when separately researched and cited;
- formal reasoning;
- synthetic data;
- simulated scenarios;
- and prototype execution records.

Unsupported claims are marked as assumptions or open questions.

---

## 12. Method 1 — Conceptual Analysis

Identify definitions, necessary and sufficient conditions, category boundaries, hidden assumptions, circular reasoning and competing interpretations.

---

## 13. Method 2 — Evidence and Source Synthesis

Trace each attributed claim to its source and distinguish evidence, interpretation and design choice.

---

## 14. Method 3 — Causal and Mechanism Mapping

Represent intervention activity, mechanism, moderator, barrier, proximal outcome and Healthy Aging outcome as separate concepts.

---

## 15. Method 4 — Ontology and Domain Modelling

Use bounded contexts, aggregates, entities, value objects, invariants, commands and events to test whether concepts can be represented without contradiction.

---

## 16. Method 5 — Formal State Analysis

Express critical lifecycles as state machines and test completeness, invalid transitions, reversibility, expiry and failure.

---

## 17. Method 6 — Synthetic Persona Analysis

Construct fictional personas with explicit assumptions to examine accessibility, support, privacy and social interaction scenarios.

Synthetic personas are not population samples and do not represent prevalence.

---

## 18. Method 7 — Synthetic Data Generation

Generate deterministic, labelled synthetic records sufficient to exercise domain and research flows.

Generators preserve provenance, seed, schema version and scenario purpose.

---

## 19. Method 8 — Simulation

Execute scenario sets across parameter variations, including normal, boundary, failure, adversarial and counterexample conditions.

---

## 20. Method 9 — Prototype Experiments

Implement non-production reference components to test whether domain rules, APIs, events, storage and UX states can be made executable.

---

## 21. Method 10 — Comparative Architecture Analysis

Compare credible alternatives and record trade-offs using Appendix C ADRs.

---

## 23. Synthetic Scenario Families

Scenario families include:

- autonomy and Consent;
- Life Story authorship;
- Visibility and audience;
- Community participation;
- matching and mutual choice;
- Connection and CommunicationBasis;
- Message lifecycle;
- Block, Report and moderation;
- SafetySignal and SafetyEvent;
- AI assistance and prohibited action;
- Dataset and analysis lineage;
- degraded dependency behaviour;
- and withdrawal/deletion propagation.

---

## 24. Formal Invariants

The programme gives priority to invariants including:

- one aggregate, one write owner;
- actor-owned MatchDecision;
- canonical MutualAcceptance source;
- single-use Connection activation;
- current CommunicationBasis before Thread/send;
- exact Message state distinctions;
- Block fail-closed behaviour;
- human authority for SafetyEvent;
- explicit AI memory creation;
- and immutable DatasetLock.

---

## 25. Life Story Research Strand

Examine identity continuity, authorship, contribution, AI Drafting, confirmation, selective sharing, correction, withdrawal and the difference between personal testimony and generated text.

---

## 26. Community Research Strand

Examine how rule versions, membership, audience, moderation, appeal, Block and social-safety constraints affect the meaning of a governed Community.

---

## 27. Matching Research Strand

Examine opt-in preferences, permitted attributes, explanations, independent decisions, mutuality, expiry, invalidation and fairness assumptions.

---

## 28. Messaging Research Strand

Examine CommunicationBasis, Thread membership, Draft, confirmation, queue, provider evidence, delivery uncertainty, Block and privacy.

---

## 29. AI Research Strand

Examine when AI assistance reduces burden, how provenance should be shown and which actions must remain human-controlled.

---

## 30. Accessibility Research Strand

Examine whether adaptation changes presentation while preserving rights, intervention meaning and measurement interpretation.

---

## 31. Research-Lineage Strand

Examine how ResearchQuestion, EvidenceDecision, Protocol, synthetic DatasetVersion, AnalysisRun, InterpretationRecord and Finding remain traceable.

---

## 32. No Human Participants

The current programme includes no human participants, recruitment, screening, consent ceremony, intervention exposure or personal follow-up.

---

## 33. No Identifiable Data

Do not use identifiable, private or confidential human data.

Any examples resembling real persons must be removed, anonymised beyond reasonable re-identification or replaced with synthetic cases.

---

## 34. No Clinical Activity

The programme does not diagnose, treat, monitor or make clinical decisions.

---

## 35. No Empirical Effect Claim

Simulation or prototype behaviour cannot establish feasibility, acceptability, effectiveness, safety or Healthy Aging benefit in real people.

---

## 36. Internal Research Review

Internal review checks clarity, source fidelity, logical validity, reproducibility, counterexamples and epistemic labelling.

It is a quality practice, not a permission gate.

---

## 37. Analysis Plan

For each ResearchQuestion:

1. identify concepts and assumptions;
2. identify source-supported statements;
3. formalise relationships;
4. construct counterexamples;
5. define synthetic scenarios;
6. execute simulations or prototype tests;
7. compare alternatives;
8. classify findings and limitations;
9. revise the model.

---

## 38. Theoretical Finding Types

A finding may be:

- coherent;
- conditionally coherent;
- underdetermined;
- contradicted;
- incomplete;
- implementation-dependent;
- source-dependent;
- or reserved for empirical testing.

---

## 39. Reproducibility

Preserve source versions, model versions, generator seeds, scenario definitions, code versions, configuration, outputs and interpretation.

---

## 40. Contradictions

A contradiction is a valuable research output.

Do not hide it through vague terminology or arbitrary implementation choices. Record affected concepts and propose alternatives.

---

## 41. Sensitivity Analysis

Vary assumptions, policy values, expiry periods, actor capabilities, failure probabilities and provider behaviour to identify fragile conclusions.

---

## 42. Negative and Adversarial Analysis

Actively search for permission bypass, hidden profiling, false delivery, automation overreach, unsafe restoration, privacy leakage and research-state compression.

---

## 43. Prototype Scope

The prototype may implement the full conceptual vertical slice with local infrastructure and mock providers.

It is an explanatory and experimental artefact, not a deployable service commitment.

---

## 44. Research Outputs

Outputs include:

- conceptual framework revisions;
- definitions and taxonomy;
- theoretical propositions;
- causal/mechanism maps;
- formal state models;
- architecture decisions;
- synthetic datasets;
- simulation reports;
- reference prototype;
- counterexample catalogue;
- and theoretical findings.

---

## 45. Completion Criteria

The current programme reaches a stable milestone when:

- key concepts are explicit;
- primary contradictions are resolved or documented;
- critical invariants are executable;
- synthetic scenarios are reproducible;
- major assumptions are classified;
- and future empirical questions are clearly separated from current findings.

---

## 46. Future Empirical Transition

A future empirical transition is optional.

It should be considered only when a specific unanswered question requires observation of real people and the conceptual model is stable enough to justify that work.

---

## 47. Current Decision

Proceed immediately with theoretical research, synthetic modelling, simulation and prototype implementation.

Do not wait for external approval, provider contracts or production infrastructure.

---

## 48. Summary

The programme is designed to produce rigorous theory and executable conceptual evidence without human-subject research.

Its central rule is:

> Use models, sources, synthetic scenarios and prototypes to sharpen the theory; label uncertainty honestly; reserve empirical claims for a separate future study.
