## 1. Purpose

This document defines the identity, purpose, boundaries, users, values, success criteria, and long-term direction of the **Healthy Aging Digital Intervention Research Platform**.

It answers the following foundational questions:

1. What are we building?
2. Why does it exist?
3. Who is it for?
4. Which challenges should it address?
5. Which principles govern it?
6. What is inside and outside the project scope?
7. What outcomes would constitute success?

Document 0 defines the ecosystem-level architecture and the responsibilities of the:

- Healthy Aging Knowledge Platform;
- Digital Intervention Research Platform;
- and AI Companion.

This document focuses on the **Digital Intervention Research Platform as a conceptual research programme and executable architecture model**.

All later product, domain, research, UX, data, AI, security, and technical specifications should remain consistent with this project definition unless this document is formally revised.

## 2. Project Name

# Healthy Aging Digital Intervention Research Platform

This is the working name for the underlying research and intervention infrastructure.

A separate public-facing product brand may be developed later without changing the platform's purpose, authority, or research model.

## 3. One-Sentence Definition

> A person-centred, evidence-informed digital intervention research platform for designing, delivering, evaluating, and improving interventions that support meaningful and healthy aging.

---

## 3.1 Current Research Mode

The current project is a **conceptual and theoretical research project**.

Its immediate work consists of:

- theory construction;
- conceptual clarification;
- evidence synthesis from supplied or explicitly researched sources;
- intervention and mechanism modelling;
- ontology and domain modelling;
- architecture reasoning;
- synthetic persona and scenario analysis;
- formal invariants and state-machine analysis;
- simulation and synthetic-data experiments;
- and non-production prototype construction.

The current phase does **not** involve:

- recruitment of human participants;
- delivery of an intervention to real people;
- collection of identifiable human-subject data;
- clinical care;
- institutional deployment;
- or claims of empirical effectiveness.

Therefore, external research ethics approval, institutional governance approval, provider approval and production-readiness approval are **not prerequisites for beginning or continuing the current theoretical research**.

References elsewhere in the Handbook to Consent, approval, Participant safety, Pilot readiness and production controls remain valuable as:

1. concepts within the architecture being studied;
2. design constraints for a hypothetical future empirical platform; and
3. criteria that would apply only if the project later transitions to real human-subject research or operational deployment.

Current research may begin immediately using public or supplied sources, synthetic data, simulated actors, mock providers and local prototypes.

## 4. Formal Definition

The Digital Intervention Research Platform is an integrated environment for:

- defining Healthy Aging challenges;
- formulating Research Questions;
- retrieving and applying evidence from the Healthy Aging Knowledge Platform;
- creating human-accountable Evidence Decisions;
- designing and versioning digital interventions;
- creating and approving Protocol Versions;
- enrolling Participants through explicit consent;
- adapting intervention delivery to individual abilities and preferences;
- supporting authorised human relationships;
- using the AI Companion within controlled boundaries;
- collecting assessments, observations, process data, outcomes, burden, and safety information;
- generating reproducible Dataset Versions;
- supporting analysis and interpretation;
- producing approved Research Findings;
- and using those findings to improve interventions and contribute to governed knowledge development.

The platform is both:

- a digital intervention delivery environment;
- and a research system for evaluating whether those interventions are useful, safe, accessible, equitable, and implementable.

## 6. What Are We Building?

The project is not primarily:

- a senior game application;
- a general-purpose AI platform;
- a generic chatbot;
- a social network;
- a telehealth system;
- a cognitive testing product;
- a care-management system;
- an electronic health record;
- a general data lake;
- or a Knowledge Graph as an end in itself.

It is:

> A platform for researching, designing, delivering, evaluating, and improving evidence-informed digital interventions for Healthy Aging.

Digital technology is treated as:

- a possible intervention medium;
- a delivery mechanism;
- a research instrument;
- and a support capability.

It is not treated as the starting point or the outcome.

## 7. The Healthy Aging Challenge

The platform should address challenges that may affect a person's ability to live a meaningful and self-directed later life.

Examples include:

- loss or reduction of meaningful social connection;
- difficulty maintaining relationships;
- barriers to community participation;
- loss of valued roles;
- reduced opportunity for contribution;
- challenges preserving and expressing identity;
- reduced confidence using digital tools;
- inaccessible digital environments;
- changes in sensory, motor, cognitive, or communication ability;
- difficulty initiating meaningful activities;
- excessive dependence on organisations or relatives for access to personal information;
- and reduced autonomy in decisions about participation, data, relationships, and daily life.

The platform should not assume that every older adult experiences the same challenge.

Every intervention should begin with a clearly defined:

- population;
- problem;
- context;
- mechanism;
- desired outcome;
- evidence basis;
- risk profile;
- and evaluation plan.

---

## 8. Core Vision

### Aging should not mean becoming disconnected from life.

The long-term vision is:

> To help people remain connected to themselves, other people, community, identity, purpose, and meaningful activity throughout later life.

More fully:

> To create a research-governed digital environment in which older adults can continue to connect, participate, express who they are, preserve and selectively share life experiences, contribute to others, exercise autonomy, and receive support adapted to their abilities and preferences.

The platform should strengthen life beyond the screen.

It should not optimise for dependency on the platform itself.

---

## 9. Core Philosophy

## 9.1 Healthy Aging Is More Than Disease Prevention

The platform does not reduce Healthy Aging to:

- absence of disease;
- longer life expectancy;
- maintenance of cognitive performance;
- physical activity alone;
- or use of health services.

Instead, it focuses on whether a person can continue to do and be what they consider meaningful and valuable.

The platform therefore considers Healthy Aging through the combined lens of:

```text
Health
+ Functional Ability
+ Relationships
+ Meaning
+ Autonomy
+ Identity
+ Participation
```

---

## 9.2 Person Before Technology

The platform does not begin with:

> What can AI or digital technology do?

It begins with:

> What challenge is the person experiencing, what intervention may help, and is digital delivery appropriate and responsible?

The preferred sequence is:

```text
Challenge
    ↓
Evidence and Theory
    ↓
Intervention
    ↓
Research Design
    ↓
Digital Delivery
    ↓
Evaluation
```

not:

```text
Technology
    ↓
Feature
    ↓
User
```

---

## 9.3 Technology Should Support Human Connection

AI and digital technology should not replace:

- family;
- friends;
- community;
- professional support;
- reciprocal relationships;
- or meaningful human presence.

Instead, technology should:

> Facilitate, strengthen, extend, or make human relationships more accessible.

Examples include:

- helping a Participant prepare to contact a friend;
- helping a family member understand what the Participant has chosen to share;
- helping people maintain relationships across distance or changes in ability;
- helping a Professional Caregiver understand approved interests and preferences;
- supporting reciprocal intergenerational communication;
- and reducing the burden of initiating a meaningful interaction.

---

## 9.4 Engagement Should Be Meaningful, Not Addictive

The platform may use:

- games;
- progress indicators;
- feedback;
- personalisation;
- curiosity;
- reminders;
- and rewards

when they support an approved intervention.

However:

> The goal is not to maximise screen time, AI conversation duration, clicks, or app-opening frequency.

The goal is:

> To increase meaningful participation and positive effects beyond the screen.

Usage metrics are process indicators only.

They are not sufficient evidence of benefit.

---

## 9.5 Research Before Claims

A digital intervention is not successful because it:

- was built;
- attracted users;
- generated fluent AI output;
- produced engagement;
- or received positive informal feedback.

It should be evaluated for:

- meaningful benefit;
- mechanism;
- feasibility;
- acceptability;
- burden;
- harm;
- accessibility;
- equity;
- fidelity;
- and implementation.

## 10. Foundational Design Principles

These principles govern product, research, UX, AI, data, and engineering decisions.

### 10.1 Person Before Technology

Start with the person's goals, needs, relationships, abilities, preferences, and context.

### 10.2 Evidence Before Claims

Claims should remain proportionate to available evidence.

Uncertainty and Knowledge Gaps must remain visible.

### 10.3 Evidence Before Intelligence

AI may retrieve, explain, compare, synthesise, and draft.

It does not replace:

- evidence;
- scientific judgement;
- or accountable human decisions.

### 10.4 Intervention Before Feature

A feature should not become part of the intervention portfolio until it has a defined:

- Healthy Aging challenge;
- target population;
- mechanism;
- intended outcome;
- evidence basis;
- risk;
- safeguard;
- and evaluation pathway.

### 10.5 Human Connection Over AI Substitution

The AI Companion should normally help users:

- connect with people;
- participate in valued activities;
- understand choices;
- navigate the platform;
- or complete an approved intervention.

It should not be designed to become a person's sole, primary, or exclusive relationship.

### 10.6 Ability Over Age

Chronological age is not a sufficient proxy for:

- sensory ability;
- motor ability;
- cognitive ability;
- communication ability;
- language;
- emotional state;
- or digital literacy.

### 10.7 Participant Ownership and Control

Participants should retain meaningful control over:

- participation;
- personal information;
- relationship access;
- supporter involvement;
- AI use;
- sharing;
- correction;
- pause;
- and withdrawal

where legally and ethically possible.

### 10.8 Consent and Minimum Necessary Access

Effective permission depends on:

```text
Role
+ Relationship
+ Consent
+ Purpose
+ Context
+ Specific Permission
+ Resource State
```

Access should be limited to the minimum necessary scope.

### 10.9 Meaningful Engagement Over Engagement Maximisation

The platform should avoid:

- coercive mechanics;
- shame;
- fear-based prompts;
- casino-like reinforcement;
- artificial urgency;
- or dependency-forming design.

### 10.10 Safety, Dignity, and Reversibility

Interventions should:

- anticipate potential harm;
- support refusal;
- support pause and withdrawal;
- provide error recovery;
- make consequential actions understandable;
- and remain reversible where possible.

### 10.11 Research-Driven Iteration

Product development should follow:

```text
Evidence
    ↓
Intervention
    ↓
Implementation
    ↓
Evaluation
    ↓
Decision
    ↓
Knowledge Improvement
```

### 10.12 Loose Coupling

The:

- Knowledge Platform;
- Research Platform;
- and AI Companion

should evolve independently through stable, governed, versioned interfaces.

Technical integration must not cause responsibility or ownership to leak across system boundaries.

### 10.13 Provenance by Default

Material:

- evidence;
- data;
- Protocols;
- Intervention Versions;
- AI configurations;
- decisions;
- datasets;
- analysis;
- and findings

should preserve source and version.

### 10.14 Human Governance

Scientific, consent, safety, privacy, intervention, dataset, and publication decisions remain under accountable human authority.

## 11. Primary Population and Participant Definition

## 11.1 Target Population

The primary population is older adults in a range of circumstances, including:

- community-dwelling older adults;
- people living independently;
- people in assisted-living settings;
- people in residential or long-term care;
- people experiencing social isolation or loneliness;
- people with different levels of digital experience;
- and people with different sensory, motor, cognitive, communication, language, and support needs.

A fundamental principle is:

> Older adults are not a homogeneous population.

The platform should not rely on one universal `Senior Mode`.

---

## 11.2 Participant

`Participant` is the canonical domain actor for a person who is:

- invited to;
- screened for;
- enrolled in;
- or interacting through

a Research Project.

A Participant may also be:

- an older adult;
- a Resident;
- a community member;
- or another person within an approved Healthy Aging Research Project.

---

## 11.3 Older Adult

`Older adult` is the broader population term.

It should not be used as a permission role.

---

## 11.4 Resident

`Resident` should be used only when the setting is:

- residential;
- assisted living;
- or long-term care.

It is not the general platform actor.

---

## 13. Core Access Principle

Access should be determined through:

```text
Effective Permission
        =
Role
+ Relationship
+ Consent
+ Purpose
+ Context
+ Specific Permission
+ Resource State
```

The following rules apply:

- a role alone does not grant unrestricted access;
- family or caregiver status alone does not grant access;
- consent does not replace specific permission;
- permission does not replace consent;
- an AI capability does not create authority;
- an administrator role does not override Participant control;
- and explicit deny overrides allow.

Document 4 is the authoritative specification for roles, relationships, consent, delegation, supported decision-making, substitute authority, purpose, context, and permission.

> A place to connect, participate, express identity, preserve and selectively share experiences, contribute, and exercise autonomy within an accessible and research-governed environment.

> A meaningful way to maintain connection without assuming ownership or unrestricted access to the Participant's information.

## 14.4 For Professional Caregivers

> A consent-aware way to support approved interventions and understand the person beyond immediate operational care tasks.

> A platform for designing, delivering, monitoring, and evaluating evidence-informed digital interventions with reproducible data and accountable governance.

## 14.6 For Intervention Designers and Developers

> A traceable environment in which product capabilities are connected to challenges, mechanisms, outcomes, evidence, risk, safeguards, permissions, data, and evaluation.

## 14.7 For Research Organisations

> A controlled infrastructure for moving from Research Question to approved Research Finding without losing consent, provenance, version history, or scientific uncertainty.

## 15. Core Product Concept

## A Participant-Centred Digital Environment for Later Life

The Participant-facing platform may include conceptual areas such as:

- **My Day** — daily plans and meaningful activity;
- **My People** — authorised family, friends, community, and care relationships;
- **My Story** — identity expression, life stories, and personal archive;
- **My Community** — structured social connection and participation;
- **My Activities** — music, learning, creativity, movement, games, projects, and interests;
- **AI Companion** — contextual assistance for access, preparation, participation, navigation, and reflection;
- **My Study** — Research Project information, consent, assessments, and participation status;
- and **My Choices** — preferences, permissions, pause, sharing, and withdrawal.

These labels are conceptual product areas.

They are not final information architecture or visual-design decisions.

The platform should feel like:

> A place that supports the Participant's choices rather than a system that takes control of them.

## 18. What the Platform Is Not

## 18.1 Not a Replacement for Healthcare

The platform is not:

- a doctor;
- nurse;
- diagnostic system;
- treatment provider;
- or emergency service.

## 18.2 Not a Replacement for Human Relationships

The AI Companion should not:

- become the Participant's only relationship;
- represent itself as human;
- imply exclusivity;
- claim emotional need;
- or manipulate continued interaction.

## 18.3 Not a Surveillance System

The platform should not be designed primarily to:

- monitor;
- score;
- rank;
- or control

older adults.

Data collection must be proportionate to an approved:

- intervention;
- safety purpose;
- operational purpose;
- or Research Question.

## 18.4 Not a Gambling Platform

Games and rewards must not depend on:

- real-money betting;
- loot boxes;
- casino-like mechanics;
- or intentionally addictive reinforcement.

## 18.5 Not a Cognitive Testing Platform by Default

Repeated memory testing is not automatically a Healthy Aging intervention.

Cognitive assessment, training, or rehabilitation requires:

- a defined intervention;
- evidence;
- appropriate governance;
- consent;
- safety;
- and evaluation.

## 18.6 Not a Repository That Removes Personal Control

Personal stories, photographs, relationships, preferences, and other personal data do not become organisational or family property merely because they are stored in the platform.

## 18.7 Not an AI-First Product

The presence of AI should not determine:

- product priorities;
- intervention design;
- research claims;
- or user relationships.

## 19. Success Criteria

For the current conceptual phase, success is demonstrated by:

- coherent and non-contradictory concepts;
- explicit causal and mechanism assumptions;
- traceability from Healthy Aging challenges to intervention propositions;
- executable domain and state models;
- reproducible synthetic scenarios and simulations;
- clear separation between theoretical findings and empirical claims;
- identification of unresolved questions and falsifiable future hypotheses;
- and a prototype that demonstrates internal architectural feasibility without pretending to be production-ready.

The current phase does not require human recruitment, external approval or real-world intervention effects.

Platform success is multidimensional.

No single usage metric is sufficient.

## 19.1 Participant-Level Success

The platform is successful when Participants can, where relevant to their goals:

- participate in valued activities;
- experience meaningful and reciprocal connection;
- express and maintain identity;
- exercise understandable choices;
- control permissions and sharing;
- use the platform with appropriate support;
- experience dignity, recognition, belonging, purpose, or autonomy;
- report concerns;
- and stop, decline, correct, pause, or withdraw without undue friction.

---

## 19.2 Relationship-Level Success

The platform supports:

- meaningful family, friendship, and community interaction;
- reciprocal rather than extractive communication;
- appropriate and trusted sharing;
- clear Supporter boundaries;
- revocable relationship access;
- and human connection rather than AI substitution.

---

## 19.3 Intervention-Level Success

Each intervention should have:

- a defined challenge;
- target population;
- explicit mechanism;
- proportionate evidence claims;
- measurable process and outcomes;
- accessibility and equity considerations;
- documented burden;
- documented risk and safeguards;
- evaluation results, including null or negative findings;
- and a decision to retain, revise, restrict, replicate, expand, suspend, retire, or continue research.

---

## 19.4 Research-Platform Success

Researchers should be able to:

- trace interventions to evidence, theory, mechanisms, outcomes, and measurements;
- identify uncertainty and Knowledge Gaps;
- create and approve Protocol Versions;
- operate Research Projects responsibly;
- preserve consent and relationship scope;
- generate reproducible Dataset Versions;
- preserve analysis lineage;
- create human-approved Research Findings;
- and prepare governed external submission packages.

---

## 19.5 Evidence-Integration Success

The platform should:

- retrieve versioned knowledge;
- preserve provenance;
- avoid uncontrolled duplication;
- preserve Evidence Decisions;
- preserve Evidence Snapshots;
- identify external evidence change;
- and prevent external changes from silently modifying approved research records.

---

## 19.6 AI Companion Success

The AI Companion is successful when it:

- assists without taking authority;
- uses authorised context only;
- grounds evidence-sensitive claims;
- identifies uncertainty;
- respects role, relationship, consent, purpose, and permission;
- requires confirmation for controlled actions;
- supports accessibility;
- supports human connection;
- and degrades safely when unavailable.

---

## 19.7 Product and Engineering Success

The platform remains:

- accessible;
- ability-adaptive;
- secure;
- privacy-preserving;
- permission-aware;
- auditable;
- modular;
- understandable;
- recoverable;
- and traceable from design decision to implementation and evaluation.

---

## 19.8 Non-Success Indicators

The following are not sufficient evidence of success:

- increased screen time;
- increased app-opening frequency;
- high click or message volume;
- longer AI conversations;
- collection of more personal data;
- rapid feature delivery;
- fluent AI output;

## 22. Design Decisions Established by This Document

This document establishes that:

1. The Digital Intervention Research Platform is the primary project.
2. Document 0 remains authoritative for ecosystem boundaries.
3. The Knowledge Platform remains independent and authoritative for knowledge governance.
4. Evidence and Knowledge Integration is an internal Research Platform capability.
5. The AI Companion is the canonical AI product and system name.
6. The AI Companion is a governed support capability, not an independent authority.
7. The project is a research and intervention platform, not a single feature or chatbot.
8. Healthy Aging is framed around meaningful life and functional ability, not disease prevention alone.
9. Intervention logic precedes product features.
10. Technology should support human connection rather than replace it.
11. Older adults are not a homogeneous population.
12. Participant is the canonical Research Platform actor.
13. Older adult is a population description.
14. Resident is a setting-specific contextual term.
15. Ability-adaptive and preference-adaptive design is foundational.
16. Participant autonomy, consent, privacy, dignity, pause, and withdrawal are core requirements.
17. Effective permission depends on role, relationship, consent, purpose, context, specific permission, and resource state.
18. Family or caregiver status does not automatically grant access.
19. Technical administration does not create research authority.
20. Engagement metrics are not equivalent to benefit.
21. Evidence strength, uncertainty, risks, and safeguards must remain visible.
22. Scientific, safety, consent, dataset, and publication decisions remain under accountable human authority.
23. The platform supports a continuous research-to-intervention-to-evidence cycle.
24. Research Findings remain distinct from authoritative Knowledge Platform publication.
25. System integration must remain loosely coupled and provenance-preserving.
26. Future expansion requires explicit evidence, authority, consent, safety, data, and evaluation.
27. Success is defined by meaningful Participant and research outcomes, not by technology use alone.

## 24. Final Project Statement

The Healthy Aging Digital Intervention Research Platform is currently a conceptual research programme and executable reference architecture.

It exists to make theories, intervention mechanisms, domain concepts, research logic and technical consequences explicit enough to examine, challenge, simulate and refine.

A future empirical study may be designed later, but it is not a prerequisite for the present work.


> The Healthy Aging Digital Intervention Research Platform is a person-centred, evidence-informed, research-governed digital environment for designing, delivering, and evaluating interventions that help older adults maintain meaningful social connection, identity, autonomy, participation, purpose, functional ability, and intergenerational relationships throughout later life.
